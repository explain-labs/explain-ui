A 3.3 kg neonate, 24 hours old, with **severe tetralogy of Fallot**. The four features:

1. a **ventricular septal defect** (VSD),
2. an **overriding aorta** above the VSD,
3. **right ventricular outflow tract (RVOT) obstruction**, critical here,
4. **right ventricular hypertrophy**.

Because the RVOT obstruction is critical, little blood reaches the lungs antegradely. The VSD shunts right-to-left into the aorta. The lungs are supplied mainly through the **open ductus arteriosus**, from the aorta to the pulmonary artery.

The VSD, the overriding aorta, the RVOT obstruction and the duct are now highlighted in the diagram.

> Look at the monitor. Compare pre- and postductal saturation, and flow through the RVOT with flow through the duct.
