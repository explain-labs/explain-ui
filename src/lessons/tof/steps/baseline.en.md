A term neonate of **3.5 kg, 72 hours old**, with **severe tetralogy of Fallot**. The four features:

1. a large **ventricular septal defect** (VSD), so both ventricles reach the same systolic pressure,
2. an **overriding aorta** above the VSD: both ventricles eject into the aorta,
3. **right ventricular outflow tract (RVOT) obstruction**, severe here,
4. a **thick-walled right ventricle**: it has pumped at systemic pressure since fetal life.

Little blood reaches the lungs through the narrow RVOT. Blue blood from the right ventricle goes into the aorta instead (a right-to-left shunt), and the lungs are supplied mainly through the **ductus arteriosus**, from the aorta to the pulmonary artery. The baby is on **prostaglandin E1**, which keeps the duct open.

The VSD, the overriding aorta, the RVOT obstruction and the duct are now highlighted in the diagram.
