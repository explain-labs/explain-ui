Figure 1 of the paper shows the main models and their causal relationships. The two lower blocks are **blood flow** (hemodynamics) and **control of circulation**; the two upper blocks are gas flow and control of breathing, described in the companion respiratory paper. Oxygen delivery, cellular metabolism and carbon-dioxide elimination sit in the centre and depend on both.

The circulation model has two sub-models:

- A **cardiac-rhythm model** that takes heart rate from the control model and generates the activation functions of the heart chambers.
- A **hemodynamics model** of cardiac and vascular volumes, pressures and flows, which responds to those activation functions, to intrathoracic pressure, and to the control effectors: contractility, arteriolar resistance and elastance, and venous unstressed volume.

Arterial pressure is fed back to the control model. The **autonomic controller** (§2.1) turns the deviation of mean arterial pressure from its set-point into a *sympatho-vagal balance* that drives heart rate, contractility, systemic vascular resistance and venous tone, each with its own gain and limits.
