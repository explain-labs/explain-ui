**What the paper did (§3.1.1).** The baseline state of the model was compared with published haemodynamic data for healthy term newborns: cardiac MRI volumes and outputs from Jhaveri et al. [17] and Groves et al. [18], superior vena cava flow from Kluckow & Evans [19], and blood pressure from van Zadelhoff et al. [20]. The reported simulation is a **3.3 kg neonate at 24 h**, sampled at end-expiration.

**How this patient was made.** The paper's own baseline was set by hand. Here the state is *re-created* by the AI parameterization pipeline: the paper's description of the case and its Table 2 were given to the Explain bot, which wrote a build specification (weight 3.3 kg, heart rate 118, arterial pressure 70/45, left-ventricular output 181 ml/kg/min, shunts closed), and the engine's calibrator fitted that specification to the term baseline. The specification and the calibration report are archived with the engine and summarised under *How this patient was built*. The language model chose the targets; it did not fit anything.

**What this run does.** It loads that built patient, settles for a minute of model time and averages the beat-to-beat read-outs over 30 s. Chamber volumes are reported per kilogram, as in Jhaveri et al.

Press **Run experiment** and compare the last column with the paper and the literature. Green means within the literature's standard deviation (or the paper's own margin where the source gives none).
