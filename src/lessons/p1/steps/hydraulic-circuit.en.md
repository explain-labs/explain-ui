Figure 2 gives the hydraulic-circuit representation of the hemodynamics model. It expands the Beneken model [12] with the tissue groups and the shunts a neonate needs:

- **Heart**: the four chambers and a separate **coronary** circulation driven by the ventricular activation function. The right atrium is implemented as two chambers (one receiving the superior vena cava, one the inferior) joined by an intra-atrial resistor, so caval streams can stay separate in the fetus; for clarity Fig. 2 draws one atrium.
- **Arteries**: ascending aorta (AA) and aortic arch (AAR) are separate, so blood of different oxygen content can reach the coronaries, the brain and the lower body — this is what makes the ductal shunt representable.
- **Organs**: brain (BR), remainder of the upper body (RUB), intestines (INT), liver and spleen (LS), kidneys (KID) and remainder of the lower body (RLB), each as arteriole, capillary bed and venule.
- **Lungs**: separate left and right lung circulations and an intrapulmonary shunt (IPS).
- **Shunts**: ductus arteriosus (DA), foramen ovale (FO), intrapulmonary shunt and, optionally, a VSD.
- **Thorax**: intra-thoracic components (blue) feel the intrathoracic pressure from breathing, ventilation and chest compressions; the pericardium limits the summed heart-chamber volume.
