The reference state of Table 3: the duct is closed. Without a ductal shunt the two ventricles pump the same output and the ratio LVO/RVO is 1.

**How this patient was made.** The bot was given the paper's description of the Bischoff case and the "no PDA" column, together with the cohort's blood pressure (45/25 mmHg) and heart rate (159 min⁻¹), which the paper's table does not list. It started from the engine's Bischoff cohort infant (25 weeks, 720 g, breathing on CPAP), closed the duct, and targeted the cohort's left- and right-ventricular output, superior vena cava flow, pressure and heart rate. The calibrator fitted the right-ventricular output through the foramen ovale size (with the duct closed, that is the only path by which the two outputs differ) and the SVC flow through the split of flow between upper and lower body. Reaching the cohort's pulse pressure needed arteries about three times stiffer than a term baby's, which the model can now integrate (the resistors between the aortic segments are computed implicitly); the calibration report under *How this patient was built* shows the fit.

The paper's conclusions from this comparison, taken from Bischoff et al. [21], are that superior vena cava flow is comparable with and without a left-to-right shunt, that left-ventricular output is higher with a left-to-right shunt, and that LVO/RVO rises with it.
