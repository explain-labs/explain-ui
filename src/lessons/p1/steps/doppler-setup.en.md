**What the paper did (§3.1.2, Fig. 5).** For a qualitative validation of the model's waveforms, the instantaneous ductal flow was transformed into a Doppler velocity with Eq. 12 — flow divided by the cross-sectional area of the duct — and compared with the transductal Doppler recordings of van Laere et al. [22] for three patterns:

- **Non-restrictive left-to-right**: pulsatile flow with a low end-diastolic velocity.
- **Restrictive left-to-right**: continuous high-velocity flow with low pulsatility, including a short early-systolic dip.
- **Bidirectional**: right-to-left in systole, left-to-right in diastole.

**What the next three runs show.** The chart under the monitor draws two velocities: the paper's Eq. 12 (bulk velocity at the pulmonary end of the duct) and the Bernoulli jet velocity a continuous-wave Doppler would read, which rises more steeply as the duct narrows. The engine latches the peak and minimum velocity of every beat; the table reports them. The recordings of van Laere et al. are in the paper itself and are not reproduced here.
