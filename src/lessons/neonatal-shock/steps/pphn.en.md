**Case 5 was PPHN.** The baby has been reset; now step by step.

A new scenario: a term baby **one hour after birth**. PVR is still high, and the duct and foramen ovale are open with **bidirectional** flow.

Press **PPHN: PVR ×3**.

[[action:pphn]]

> Compare the pre- and post-ductal saturations. Which way do the arrows in the duct and the foramen ovale run now (diagram)?

[[reveal]]

**Differential cyanosis**: deoxygenated blood goes right-to-left through the duct into the descending aorta. Watch **DO₂ lower body**: it is fed by that post-ductal blood. Left ventricular output falls, because less blood passes through the lungs to the left atrium.

Support the systemic side: **Increase SVR (×1.5)** (what a vasopressor does).

[[action:svr-up]]

> What happens in the duct (diagram) and to the post-ductal saturation?

[[reveal]]

The direction of the shunt depends on the **ratio of SVR to PVR**. Raising SVR cuts the right-to-left duct flow to about a third and the post-ductal saturation rises a little (71 → 77 %). Systemic vasodilators do the opposite.

Now treat the cause: **Lower PVR** (stands for oxygen, ventilation and iNO).

[[action:lower-pvr]]

> What happens to the saturations, the LV output and the duct?

[[reveal]]

**Is the open duct good or bad in PPHN?** Press **Severe PPHN: PVR ×5**, and then **Close the duct**. Fast-forward a couple of minutes.

[[action:severe,close-duct,wait-2]]

> At first the closed duct even looks better. Why? Then look at RV output, CVP and MAP.

[[reveal]]

As long as the right ventricle copes, a right-to-left duct mostly costs saturation. Once the RV reaches its limit, the duct is its **pop-off valve**: closing it makes the RV fail. That is why in severe PPHN with RV failure the duct is sometimes kept open with **PGE1**.

You can also move the duct yourself under **Interventions**.
