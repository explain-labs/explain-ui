**Case 7 was pulmonary overcirculation in HLHS.** The baby has been reset; now step by step.

In HLHS the left heart cannot pump. The **right ventricle is the only pump**: it fills the lungs and, through the **duct**, the whole body (the arch and coronaries backwards). All blood from the lungs returns across the **atrial septum** and mixes with the body's venous blood. Lungs and body are **parallel circulations**, and the split between them (Qp:Qs) is set by the ratio of **PVR to SVR**.

Press **Pulmonary vascular resistance falls**, then **Fast-forward 5 min** (about half a minute of computing).

[[action:pvr-falls,wait-5]]

> The saturation rises. Is the baby better? Look at the duct flow, the RV output, the diastolic pressure, DO₂ lower body and the lactate.

[[reveal]]

The saturation rises from about 77 to 89 %, but the duct now carries much less to the body (systemic flow almost halved) while the RV pumps more: the extra output goes to the **lungs** (Qp:Qs about 4). The **diastolic pressure falls** (about 29: blood runs off into the low-resistance lungs during diastole), the lower body gets about half the oxygen, and the **lactate rises**. **In HLHS a high saturation is a warning sign**: it means too much pulmonary flow.

Many reflexes would reach for a vasopressor. Try **Increase SVR (×1.5)**, and fast-forward a few minutes.

[[action:svr-up]]

> What happens to the systemic flow and the lactate?

[[reveal]]

It gets **worse**: with a higher SVR the RV output shifts even more to the lungs (Qp:Qs about 6), the systemic flow falls further and the lactate climbs (about 4). In a parallel circulation, raising SVR steals from the body.

Now the right target from the same starting point. Press **Repeat the overcirculation** (about half a minute of computing), then **Increase PVR (×1.5)**, twice, and fast-forward a few minutes.

[[action:replay]]

[[action:pvr-up]]

> Compare: saturation, systemic flow, diastolic pressure, lactate.

[[reveal]]

The saturation falls back (about 84 %), but the body gets more (systemic flow about +40 %), the diastolic pressure recovers and the **lactate falls**. The target was **PVR** (the balance Qp:Qs). In practice: no extra oxygen (room air), no hyperventilation (permissive hypercapnia), sometimes hypoxic or CO₂-enriched gas; and avoid vasopressors that raise SVR. Lowering SVR (afterload reduction, e.g. milrinone) helps the systemic side a little; more contractility mostly feeds the lungs.
