**Case 7 was warm septic shock**, the distributive type. The baby has been reset; now step by step.

The other phenotype: **warm** septic shock. The arterioles dilate deeply (**SVR falls**), the capillaries **leak**, the fever raises the oxygen demand, and leak and poor intake have cost about a fifth of the blood volume.

Press **Warm septic shock**, then **Fast-forward 5 min** (about half a minute of computing).

[[action:sepsis,wait-5]]

> Look at the heart rate, the blood pressure (and its diastolic part), the LV output, SvO₂, lactate and the diuresis. What is the problem here?

[[reveal]]

The heart rate is about 190 and the pressure low (MAP about 37, **diastolic about 31**), but the **output is high** (about 285 ml/kg/min) and SvO₂ is fine: **warm shock**. The problem is **perfusion pressure**, not total flow. The organ that shows it is the **kidney**: the **diuresis falls from about 1.6 to well below 1 ml/kg/h** (it fluctuates from moment to moment; watch it for a while).

The **lactate stays normal** in this model. Its tissues only make lactate when the oxygen delivered falls short, and the high output prevents that (even with far deeper vasodilation and a doubled oxygen demand). In a real septic baby the lactate rises anyway, through **maldistribution** (blood bypasses the tissues in the microcirculation) and **cellular dysfunction** (the cells cannot use the oxygen). A normal lactate does not exclude septic shock.

Now give **Bolus NaCl 0.9 % 20 ml/kg**, then press **Fast-forward 2 min**. Watch **Blood volume**, **Interstitial fluid** and the pressure.

[[action:bolus,wait-2]]

> Where did the bolus go, and what did it do to the pressure?

[[reveal]]

The blood volume rises (about 70 → 86 ml/kg) and falls back within minutes (about 75): the bolus ends up in the **interstitium** (the leak is exaggerated so it fits the session). The pressure hardly changes: the vessels are too wide for the volume to raise it. In the patient this becomes oedema, worse lung compliance and a positive fluid balance.

Now treat the target that failed: **Increase SVR (×2)** (what a vasopressor such as noradrenaline does), and fast-forward a few minutes.

[[action:svr-up]]

> What happens to the pressure, the LV output and the diuresis?

[[reveal]]

The pressure rises (MAP about 48), the output falls back from its hyperdynamic level, and the **diuresis recovers** (back to above 1 ml/kg/h). Press it a second time: MAP about 62, but now the **CVP goes below zero**, because the restored tone shows how much volume was lost: this baby also needs careful volume. The targets were **SVR** and **leak**: restore tone, give volume carefully. Compare with the cold shock, where the same lever made things worse.
