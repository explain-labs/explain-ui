**Case 3 was warm septic shock**, the distributive type. The baby has been reset; now step by step.

The other phenotype: **warm** septic shock. Two things happen at the same time: the arterioles dilate (**SVR falls**) and the capillaries start to **leak**.

Press **Sepsis: vasodilation + capillary leak**.

[[action:sepsis]]

> The blood pressure falls. What does the cardiac output do? What about SvO₂?

[[reveal]]

This is **warm shock**: output is high, SvO₂ is normal and the lower-body delivery even rises. The problem here is **perfusion pressure** (coronaries, brain, kidneys), not total flow. In real sepsis, maldistribution and cellular dysfunction also play a role: the model does not show that.

Now give **Bolus NaCl 0.9 % 20 ml/kg**, then press **Fast-forward 2 min**. Watch **Blood volume** and **Interstitial fluid**.

[[action:bolus,wait-2]]

> Where did the bolus go?

[[reveal]]

With a leak, a bolus stays in the circulation only briefly. Here it is gone within a few minutes (the leak is exaggerated so it fits the session). In the patient this becomes oedema, worse lung compliance and a positive fluid balance.

Now treat the target that failed: **Increase SVR (×2)** (what a vasopressor such as noradrenaline does).

[[action:svr-up]]

> What happens to the pressure, the LV output and SvO₂? Why does this target fit here better than more fluid?

[[reveal]]

The pressure returns to normal (about 56) and the LV output falls back from its hyperdynamic level (about 260 → 180) while SvO₂ stays fine: tone restored, no extra volume needed. The targets were **SVR** and **leak**: restore tone, give volume carefully. Compare with the cold shock, where the same lever made things worse.
