A term baby, 3 days old, with **neonatal sepsis**. In newborns septic shock is often **cold**: the infection depresses the myocardium, the vessels constrict, and the capillaries leak.

Press **Cold septic shock**, then **Fast-forward 2 min**.

[[action:cold-sepsis,wait-2]]

> Look at the blood pressure, the pulse pressure (on the monitor), the LV output and the SvO₂. Is this baby in shock?

[[reveal]]

The **blood pressure barely falls**, but the LV output is about **halved**, the pulse pressure is narrow, SvO₂ falls to the mid-60s and the lower body gets about 45 % less oxygen. The baroreflex and the vasoconstriction keep the pressure up; the flow is gone. **A normal blood pressure does not rule out shock.**

Give **Bolus NaCl 0.9 % 10 ml/kg**.

[[action:bolus]]

> What does the bolus do, and how long does it last?

[[reveal]]

Very little, and only briefly: the leak takes it out of the circulation, and a weak heart cannot do much with the extra preload.

Now a drug. Start with **Noradrenaline 0.3 mcg/kg/min**.

[[action:noradrenaline]]

> The pressure rises. Does the flow? Look at the LV output, SvO₂ and DO₂ lower body.

[[reveal]]

Noradrenaline raises the pressure (to about 62) but the LV output hardly (+15 %): it adds afterload to a heart that cannot cope with it.

Now try the other drug from the same starting point. Press **Repeat the cold sepsis** (the baby is reset and the sepsis runs for 4 minutes, computed in one go: about half a minute), then start **Adrenaline 0.1 mcg/kg/min**.

[[action:replay]]

[[action:adrenaline]]

> Compare with noradrenaline: pressure, LV output, SvO₂, DO₂ lower body.

[[reveal]]

Adrenaline raises the pressure (about 66) **and** the flow (LV output +37 %, SvO₂ about 72 %), because it acts on the target that failed: **contractility**. That is why cold shock is treated with an inotrope (adrenaline; dobutamine or milrinone, not in the model) and warm shock with a vasopressor (noradrenaline), as in the next step.

In this model the lactate stays normal: the delivery does not fall low enough. In a real baby, a rising lactate would be expected.
