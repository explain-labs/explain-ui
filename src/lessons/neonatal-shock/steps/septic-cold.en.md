**Case 2 was cold septic shock.** The baby has been reset; now step by step.

A term baby, 3 days old, who has been unwell for hours with **neonatal sepsis**. In newborns septic shock is often **cold**: the infection depresses the myocardium, the vessels constrict, the capillaries leak, and fever raises the oxygen demand. Leak and poor intake have already cost about a fifth of the blood volume.

Press **Cold septic shock**, then **Fast-forward 5 min** (about half a minute of computing).

[[action:cold-sepsis,wait-5]]

> Look at the heart rate, blood pressure, pulse pressure (on the monitor), LV output, SvO₂, lactate and BE. Compensated or not?

[[reveal]]

**Decompensated.** The heart rate is about 190, the LV output less than half of normal, SvO₂ around 40 %, and lactate keeps **rising** (about 4–5 mmol/l) with a growing metabolic acidosis. The mean pressure is still about 49 (low for 3 days old) with a **pulse pressure of only ~8 mmHg**: the vasoconstriction holds the pressure while the flow is gone. Hypotension is a late sign in neonates.

Give **Bolus NaCl 0.9 % 10 ml/kg**.

[[action:bolus]]

> What does the bolus do, and what does it cost?

[[reveal]]

A little: the heart rate falls somewhat and the output rises about 10 %, but the leak takes the fluid away, the weak heart cannot use the preload, and the saline deepens the acidosis (BE falls by about 3).

Now treat a **target**. First the pressure: **Increase SVR (×1.5)** (what a vasopressor does). Fast-forward a few minutes if you like.

[[action:svr-up]]

> The pressure rises. Does the flow? And SvO₂ and the lactate?

[[reveal]]

The pressure rises a little, but the LV output **falls** (about 100 → 70), SvO₂ drops further (about 33 %) and the **lactate keeps rising** (about 6). More afterload for a heart that cannot cope with it: a better number, a sicker baby.

Now the other target from the same starting point. Press **Repeat the cold sepsis** (reset, 5 minutes of sepsis, the bolus and 2 more minutes, computed in one go: about 45 seconds), then **Increase contractility (×2)** (what an inotrope does).

[[action:replay]]

[[action:contractility-up]]

> Compare with the SVR: pressure, LV output, SvO₂, lactate.

[[reveal]]

Pressure **and** flow rise (MAP about 60, LV output about 140, SvO₂ about 57 %), and the **lactate starts to fall**. This is the target that failed: **contractility**. In practice: an inotrope (adrenaline, dobutamine, milrinone), not a pure vasopressor. In warm shock, the last case of this lesson, it is the other way round.
