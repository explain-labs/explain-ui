| Phenotype | What fails | Clues | Primary target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypovolaemic | Preload | Tachycardia, low CVP, falling SvO₂; BP late | Volume (blood if bleeding) |
| Septic, cold | Contractility, leak (SVR high) | Near-normal MAP, narrow pulse pressure, low output, low SvO₂ | Contractility up (inotrope: adrenaline, dobutamine, milrinone); careful volume. Raising SVR makes it worse |
| Septic, warm (distributive) | SVR, leak | Low MAP and low diastolic pressure, high output, normal SvO₂ | SVR up (vasopressor: noradrenaline, vasopressin); careful volume |
| Cardiogenic, systolic | Contractility | High CVP, small SV, poor response to fluid | Inotrope, afterload, cause |
| Cardiogenic, diastolic | Filling | High CVP, small SV, fluid raises the CVP steeply | Careful volume, rate, lusitropy |
| PPHN | PVR (RV afterload) | Pre/post SpO₂ gap, R→L shunts | Lower PVR, keep SVR > PVR, the duct as pop-off |

Three messages:

1. **Septic shock is a mixture.** In one baby you will find a bit of every row: vasodilation, leak, myocardial depression and raised PVR. Analyse which term dominates *now*. It changes over hours.
2. **Blood pressure is a late and incomplete marker.** Combine heart rate, CVP, SvO₂, lactate, saturation difference and echo.
3. **The duct cuts both ways.** It is life-saving in duct-dependent lesions: systemic flow (HLHS, critical coarctation, interrupted arch: shock in the first two weeks = think of the duct and start PGE1), pulmonary flow (pulmonary atresia) and as an RV pop-off. A large left-to-right duct, on the other hand, can steal systemic flow, especially in preterms.

Use the **Interventions** tab to combine the levers yourself (for example lower contractility, raise PVR and lower SVR) and build your own septic baby.
