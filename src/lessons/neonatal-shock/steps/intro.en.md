Shock is not a blood pressure. Shock is **too little oxygen delivery (DO₂) for what the tissues need**.

**DO₂ = cardiac output × arterial O₂ content**, and **cardiac output = heart rate × stroke volume**.

Stroke volume depends on **preload** (volume, venous tone, capillary leak), **contractility**, **diastolic function** (can the ventricle fill?) and **afterload** (SVR for the left ventricle, PVR for the right). In the newborn the **ductus arteriosus** and the **foramen ovale** connect the two sides and can change everything.

Every treatment you give acts on one of these terms. That is the idea of this session: **find the term that fails, and treat that term.**

What is special about the neonate:

- Little contractile reserve: a stiff, immature myocardium. Output depends heavily on **heart rate**.
- High and labile **PVR** in the first days, with open fetal channels.
- **Blood pressure is a late marker.** Look at heart rate, CVP, SvO₂ and lactate as well.

Find these on the right: **LV stroke volume**, **LV output**, **SvO₂** (mixed venous saturation: falls when delivery falls behind demand), **lactate**, and the oxygen delivery to the **brain** and the **lower body** (ml O₂/min; the lower body is an estimate from renal flow). This is a healthy term baby, 3 days old, 3.5 kg. Remember the numbers.
