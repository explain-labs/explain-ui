Now a ventricle that contracts well but **cannot fill**: a **stiff ventricle**. Think of the hypertrophic heart of the infant of a diabetic mother, or a heart after asphyxia.

Press **Stiff ventricles**.

> The stroke volume falls, but the EDP stays high. Compare this with the weak heart: what is the difference?

Give **Bolus NaCl 0.9 % 10 ml/kg**.

> What happens to the EDP? How much extra output do you get?

A stiff ventricle turns a little extra volume into a lot of extra pressure: **the filling pressure rises steeply**, and the lungs pay the price.

Now try **Heart rate 160**, and then **Heart rate 100**.

> In this model a faster heart rate *helps*. Why?

The stiffness here is **passive** (restrictive physiology). The stroke volume is fixed, so output follows the heart rate. That is different when **active relaxation** is slow, or in **hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with outflow obstruction**: there, tachycardia shortens filling and makes things worse, and the treatment is slower rate, volume and avoiding inotropes. The model does not show that type.

The target was **diastolic function**: careful with volume, and watch the rate.
