A term baby after a difficult vacuum extraction. The head circumference is increasing: a **subgaleal bleed**. The patient has just been reset to a healthy baby.

Press **Subgaleal bleed**. The baby loses 35 % of its blood volume over 2 minutes (in reality this usually takes longer: the clock is compressed).

> Watch the order of events. What changes first: heart rate, CVP, SvO₂ or blood pressure?

Press **Fast-forward 2 min**.

> The blood pressure comes back up a little by itself. Is the baby getting better? Look at heart rate, SvO₂ and lactate.

The **baroreflex** defends the pressure with tachycardia and vasoconstriction, *at the cost of* flow. A recovering BP in a tachycardic baby with a falling SvO₂ is compensated shock, not recovery.

> Compare **DO₂ brain** and **DO₂ lower body**. Which falls more?

The brain is spared (delivery falls by about 40 %) while the lower body loses about 80 %: vasoconstriction redistributes the flow that is left. This is why gut and kidneys are the first casualties (NEC, oliguria) and why a normal-looking brain does not mean the baby is fine.

Now give **Bolus NaCl 0.9 % 20 ml/kg**.

> What happens to the CVP, the stroke volume and the heart rate? And to the **BE**?

The target was **preload**: the volume came back, and so did stroke volume. The fall in BE is the **hyperchloraemic acidosis** of saline. For a large haemorrhage the right fluid is blood.
