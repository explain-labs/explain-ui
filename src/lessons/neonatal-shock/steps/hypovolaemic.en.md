**Case 1 was hypovolaemic shock** from a subgaleal bleed. The baby has been reset; now step by step.

A term baby, **12 hours old**, after a difficult vacuum extraction. The head circumference is increasing: a **subgaleal bleed**. The patient has just been reset to a healthy baby of that age: the **duct is still half open**, with left-to-right flow.

Press **Subgaleal bleed**. The baby loses 35 % of its blood volume over 2 minutes (in reality this usually takes longer: the clock is compressed). Don't want to wait? **Fast-forward 2 min** jumps to the end of the bleed.

[[action:bleed]]

[[action:wait-2]]

> Watch the order of events. What changes first: heart rate, CVP, SvO₂ or blood pressure?

[[reveal]]

Press **Fast-forward 2 min**.

[[action:wait-2]]

> The blood pressure comes back up a little by itself. Is the baby getting better? Look at heart rate, SvO₂ and lactate.

[[reveal]]

The **baroreflex** defends the pressure with tachycardia and vasoconstriction, *at the cost of* flow. A recovering BP in a tachycardic baby with a falling SvO₂ is compensated shock, not recovery.

> Compare **DO₂ brain** and **DO₂ lower body**. Which falls more?

[[reveal]]

The lower body loses most (delivery falls by about 85 %): vasoconstriction redistributes the flow that is left toward the brain. This is why gut and kidneys are the first casualties (NEC, oliguria).

> And the duct? Look at its flow and compare it with the LV output.

[[reveal]]

The duct stays open **left-to-right**: of an LV output of about 100 ml/kg/min, some 40 go back into the lungs. In the first day of life the body gets even less than the LV output suggests, which is why the brain is hit harder here (delivery about −55 %) than in an older baby with a closed duct (about −40 %).

Now give **Bolus NaCl 0.9 % 20 ml/kg**.

[[action:bolus]]

> What happens to the CVP, the stroke volume and the heart rate? And to the **BE**?

[[reveal]]

The target was **preload**: the volume came back, and so did stroke volume. The fall in BE is the **hyperchloraemic acidosis** of saline. And look at the **Hb**: it fell from 10 to about 7 mmol/l. The heart now pumps more blood with less oxygen in it.

Now the same bleed, treated with blood. Press **Repeat the bleed**: the baby is reset, bleeds 35 % and the 2 minutes pass, computed in one go (this takes about half a minute). You arrive at the moment you gave the saline.

[[action:replay]]

Then press **Transfuse packed red cells 20 ml/kg**.

[[action:transfuse]]

> Compare with the saline bolus a few minutes later: Hb, BE, LV output, heart rate and DO₂.

[[reveal]]

After the transfusion the Hb is back to normal and there is no acidosis. The heart delivers *more* oxygen while pumping about 20 % *less* (LV output about 210 against 265 ml/kg/min after saline). In a large haemorrhage, blood replaces both volume and oxygen-carrying capacity; crystalloid only replaces volume.
